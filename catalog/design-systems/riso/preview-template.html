Manifesto / single-page essay

Print loudly.
Edit quietly.

The cheapest way to be opinionated on the internet is to print a zine, scan it, and post the JPEG. Riso lets you skip the scanner.

// Section 01

Constraints become the identity.

Two spot colours. No grey ramp. Zero radius. Chunky display at weight 700 with leading 1.0. The risograph printer in the basement had three ink drums and a temperamental drum cleaner — the operator decided which constraints to fight and which to let define the issue.

Body copy

Sans for legibility, serif for shout.

The body is system sans at 17px with 1.55 leading — generous enough to read a 1,200-word essay without strain. The display serif is chunky enough to anchor a 4.5rem headline at the top of a print-shape page without looking spindly. Mixing weights between display and body is the slow-pace signal that says this took someone an afternoon to write, not five minutes to prompt-engineer.

// Aside

Riso skipped the muted-grey ramp entirely. Every grey beat is warm-tinted near-paper or near-print. The two-spot constraint extends into the chrome of the page itself — the same restraint, all the way down.

// Three principles for an opinion-loaded artifact
01

Don't apologise.

If the page can't hold its position, the position wasn't the right one. The chrome shouldn't soften the prose.

02

Pick one shout.

The pink is the voltage. The teal grounds it. A third colour would dilute both — print a second zine instead.

03

Edit on paper.

Drafts in a doc, final pass on a printed proof. The medium changes which sentences feel right.
