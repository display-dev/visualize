# Superhuman Style

_Independent reference inspired by Superhuman's public product and help surfaces. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Superhuman._

## §1 Canonical Canvas

Superhuman style is premium productivity: fast email, AI-native workflows, split inboxes, precise reminders, shared conversations, team comments, snippets, calendar context, read statuses, and now broader Superhuman suite signals. It should feel calm and expensive, but never vague. The design is about moving through work at speed with control.

Use a dark indigo editorial hero, white or warm off-white product sections, refined typography, tight rounded buttons, screenshots of inbox and calendar states, and a deep teal closing band. Avoid app-store busyness. The product promise is not "more email"; it is faster decisions and fewer missed follow-ups.

### global-header

Use a refined header with Products, Mail, AI, Teams, Resources, Pricing, Sign in, and Get started. Keep it quiet, with restrained spacing and one strong CTA.

### productive-email-hero

The hero should state the core promise plainly: productive email, AI-native inbox, or fastest email for high-performing teams. Pair copy with an inbox/calendar composite, not abstract AI art.

### inbox-command-center

Show the main mail surface: message list, reading pane, command bar, split inbox tabs, keyboard hints, calendar side panel, and quick actions. Keep the UI airy but information-rich.

### split-inbox-panel

Split Inbox is a signature component. Show Important, Other, Team, VIP, News, Calendar, Reminders, custom auto labels, and tool-specific splits. The visual must communicate focus, not folders.

### ask-ai-card

Ask AI answers questions across inbox, calendar, and web. Show a prompt, grounded answer, source messages, and next action. Keep AI elegant and accountable.

### ai-draft-card

AI writing cards turn bullet points into email, adjust tone, summarize threads, and prepare replies. Pair the generated draft with recipient context and an edit affordance.

### auto-label-card

Auto Labels use short AI prompts to sort incoming mail. Show condition, label, preview matches, and review controls. This is automation with human oversight.

### reminders-card

Reminders bring messages back if no one replies or at a chosen time. Show date, condition, thread, and follow-up draft. The state should feel precise.

### send-later-card

Send Later cards show delivery time, recipient timezone, and schedule confirmation. Include calendar awareness when relevant.

### read-status-card

Read Status cards show opened, device, time, teammate drafting, and thread activity. Use subtle badges, not surveillance-heavy visuals.

### snippets-card

Snippets are reusable email blocks with variables, attachments, CCs, and team sharing. Show typed trigger, preview, and inserted result.

### calendar-sidecar

Calendar is always near email. Show daily agenda, availability, meeting proposal, and scheduling action without leaving the inbox.

### shared-conversation-card

Shared Conversations show a live email view, team comments, assignees, and internal discussion without forwarding. Use comment markers and permissions.

### team-comments-card

Team comments are small, private, and contextual. Show a comment thread attached to an email line or decision point.

### team-read-status-card

Team read statuses prevent duplicate work. Show teammate already replying, assigned owner, and activity state.

### mcp-server-card

The Superhuman Mail MCP surface should show an agent retrieving messages, checking reminders, drafting follow-up, or searching calendar context with permission boundaries.

### keyboard-shortcuts-card

Keyboard shortcuts are core to speed. Show a compact command palette with shortcuts for inbox, important, reminders, snippets, calendar, search, archive, and reply.

### command-palette

The command palette is a dark or light overlay with fuzzy search, action labels, and shortcut hints. It should feel instant.

### search-card

Search finds exact messages, attachments, conversations, people, and dates. With AI, it should accept natural language and show source-backed results.

### unsubscribe-card

Unsubscribe and clean-up actions should be calm and decisive: sender, volume, last opened, unsubscribe action, and archive choice.

### mobile-inbox-card

Mobile views preserve speed: split inbox, swipe actions, AI summary, calendar, and quick reply. Keep mobile polished and uncluttered.

### onboarding-white-glove-card

Superhuman has a premium onboarding reputation. Show setup, shortcuts, inbox method, calendar, team features, and coaching checklist.

### suite-card

Superhuman Suite includes more than Mail. Show Mail, AI, Go, Grammarly, Coda, or suite-level capabilities as an integrated productivity surface without diluting the mail identity.

### pricing-plan-card

Pricing cards show plan name, seat price, AI access, team features, shared snippets, read statuses, comments, support, and enterprise controls. Keep it sober and direct.

### feature-comparison-table

Tables compare AI chat, inline suggestions, Split Inbox, reminders, read statuses, calendar, snippets, shared conversations, team comments, MCP, admin, support, and early access.

### testimonial-panel

Testimonials should be polished and minimal, with a short quote about hours saved, faster replies, or better follow-through. Use executive/productivity credibility.

#### update-card

Update cards from the changelog/news surface show feature name, date, product area, and short description. Keep badges restrained.

### closing-teal-band

The closing CTA uses a deep teal or indigo-green band with white type and one button. This is the emotional punctuation after white product sections.

#### footer-minimal

Footer groups Products, Resources, Company, Security, Pricing, Download, and Support. Keep it smaller than the premium hero.

## §2 Palette

The palette is dark indigo, warm white, muted ink, violet atmosphere, and deep teal. It is premium productivity, not generic purple SaaS.

#### indigo-hero

Use deep indigo for hero, featured pricing, and high-emphasis panels. It should feel nocturnal and focused.

#### warm-white

Use white and warm off-white for content, pricing, and inbox demonstrations. Keep the body calm.

#### deep-teal

Use deep teal as a closing or conversion band, not as the primary brand color everywhere.

#### subtle-violet

Violet can appear as atmospheric hero light or soft surface tint. Avoid bright lavender UI controls.

## §3 Typography

Typography should feel premium and fast: large, tight, and clear.

#### display-type

Use a polished variable sans with medium-heavy weight and tight line-height. Headlines are concise.

#### body-type

Body copy is understated and confident. Avoid productivity clichés and inflated AI promises.

#### shortcut-type

Shortcuts and command labels can use compact mono-like alignment or tabular spacing, but keep the same sans family if possible.

## §4 Composition Rules

Every composition should reduce inbox stress and increase speed.

### inbox-before-claims

Show actual inbox states before abstract productivity statements.

### one-premium-gesture

Use one atmospheric hero, one deep CTA band, or one editorial portrait. Do not stack multiple premium effects.

### speed-through-controls

Communicate speed with shortcuts, command palette, reminders, and AI action buttons, not motion blur or lightning icons.

### team-context-visible

When showing team workflows, show comments, owners, read status, and shared conversation boundaries.

### calendar-near-email

Calendar should appear close to email because scheduling is part of the workflow.

## §5 Accessibility And States

Premium UI still needs clear states. The audience moves fast; ambiguity causes missed work.

### focus-state

Focus rings use indigo or teal outlines with enough contrast on white and dark surfaces.

### priority-state

Priority states use labels: Important, VIP, Team, Reminder, Waiting, Read, Drafting. Do not rely on color alone.

### ai-review-state

AI drafts and labels need review affordances, sources, and edit controls.

### loading-state

Loading states should resemble inbox rows, summaries, calendar slots, or command results.

## §6 Anti-Patterns

Superhuman style fails when it becomes generic AI email or noisy collaboration.

### avoid-inbox-chaos

Do not show overloaded messy inboxes without a clear triage system.

### avoid-ai-sparkle

AI must be tied to drafts, search, summaries, labels, reminders, or scheduling.

### avoid-cheap-premium

Do not use gold gradients, luxury serif tropes, or generic dark glass cards.

### avoid-casual-chat

Superhuman is email productivity, not social messaging. Threads, comments, and shared conversations must retain email context.
