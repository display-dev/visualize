# Brand · Andreessen Horowitz

## What this is

A venture capital firm publishing essays, theses, research reports, and podcast content on technology markets. The public surface is essays ("Software Is Eating the World", "It's Time to Build", "Why AI Will Save the World"), podcast transcripts (a16z Podcast, AI + a16z, Web3 with a16z), data-driven research reports (State of Crypto, State of AI, the Unicorn studies), and portfolio announcements. The firm's job-to-be-done on the public surface is *intellectual positioning* — shaping the conversation founders, regulators, and the press are having about the sectors the firm invests in.

## Voice

**Axis: analytical-structural** (data-cited, framework-grounded, position-forward).

Sentences claim the position upfront and back it with the data point or framework. Marc Andreessen's solo essays sit at the most-amplified end of the register; portfolio announcements sit at the most-restrained. Research reports carry the heaviest data load (percentile breakdowns, year-over-year cohort analysis) with prose that interprets rather than reasserts.

When `voice` choices arise: prefer the structural framing ("the unicorn 90th-percentile compounds at 6× the median") over the rhetorical one ("top performers are dramatically ahead"). Cite the cohort, the percentile, the source.

## Audience

- **Primary**: founders the firm wants to back, founders in portfolio companies, the broader tech industry tracking the firm's thesis and research.
- **Secondary**: limited partners, regulators, the press, the public absorbing tech narrative.

The primary read surface is the firm's essay site (a16z.com), the podcast feeds, and the standalone reports (state-of-* PDF + web microsite). Twitter / X carries the same content compressed.

## Format default

**presentation** — most public artifacts are presentation-shaped: a thesis essay with a big-claim H1, supporting sections, big-number tiles, charts. Research reports lean toward publication register (serif headings, multi-hue chart palette) but the underlying format is still presentation-ordered (one concept per section, big visual claims).

## Exceptions

- The brand commits to positions some readers will disagree with. Don't sand the assertion into a both-sides framing in `polish` — the thesis is the artifact.
- Cite founder + company names directly when invoking a portfolio example. Generic founder anecdotes read off-brand.
- Reports carry their own typographic register (serif title, cream background) — that's a sub-brand decision, not a slop violation; the detector should not flag it.
