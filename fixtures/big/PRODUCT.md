# Brand · Bjarke Ingels Group

## What this is

An architecture, urbanism, and design practice founded by Bjarke Ingels. The firm publishes case studies of completed and in-progress projects (LEGO House, 8 House, Two World Trade Center, Mars Science City, masterplans across Toronto, Copenhagen, Brooklyn), concept-stage explorations, and a small set of bestselling books that double as monographs (*Yes Is More*, *Hot to Cold*, *Formgiving*). The public surface is project case studies and conceptual essays.

## Voice

**Axis: pragmatic-visionary** (explanatory-then-poetic; the case-study spine carries dimensions, programs, materials, then ends with the philosophical claim).

Sentences open with the building program ("eight stories of residential atop two of retail"), build through the design constraints, and close with the conceptual move that resolves them. The firm's signature phrase is "Yes Is More" — the rhetorical posture is *additive*, not subtractive: every constraint is reframed as an opportunity for design synthesis.

When `voice` choices arise: prefer the explanatory framing of the design problem over the marketing framing of the result. *"The northern facade had to capture morning light without east-side noise; the angled glazing solves both"* beats *"a striking sustainable facade"*.

## Audience

- **Primary**: clients (developers, municipalities, institutions evaluating BIG for a commission), planning departments reviewing applications, fellow architects and design students.
- **Secondary**: the design press, the general public absorbing the firm's public-realm work, BIG-curious students considering architecture school.

The primary public surface is the firm's project case studies (big.dk) and the firm's books. Lectures and conference talks carry the brand to design school audiences.

## Format default

**technical-drawing** — most artifacts are technical-drawing-shaped: case studies with axonometric drawings, plan and section pairs, conceptual diagrams, dimensioned facade studies. Reports for planning departments lean Whitepaper register; the default register stays Blueprint.

## Exceptions

- Diagram callouts and conceptual annotations are first-class content, not chrome. Don't strip them in `simplify` — they carry the architectural argument.
- Project naming is canonical (`8 House`, `LEGO House`) — don't generalize to "the residential project" in iteration verbs.
