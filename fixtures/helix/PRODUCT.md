# Brand · Helix

## What this is

Open-source developer-tool company shipping a single CLI (`helix`) for refactoring legacy codebases through structured-edit primitives. The product is a precise, opinionated tool — not a platform, not a framework, not a workflow. Audience is staff and senior engineers maintaining large brownfield codebases.

## Voice

**Axis: raw / opinionated** (manifesto register).

Short sentences. Direct claims. No hedging where the team has a position; the team has positions on most things. Profanity is fine when it lands; avoided when it doesn't. No marketing-shaped prose ("trusted by", "loved by", "join thousands"). The brand earns trust by being right about hard problems, not by softening edges.

When `voice` choices arise (Postmortem reflection, FAQ phrasings, Plan-review prose): keep the edge. A Postmortem that reads like a corporate apology is off-brand; a Postmortem that says "we missed this because we trusted the tests and the tests were lying" is on-brand.

## Audience

- **Primary**: staff engineers, principal engineers, tech-lead-managers running large refactoring efforts.
- **Secondary**: VPEs choosing tools; OSS contributors.

Primary audience reads on GitHub READMEs, in terminals (man pages), and in CI output. They run the CLI before they read the docs. The brand surfaces follow the same priority: code-first, prose-second.

## Format default

**code-shop** — most artifacts are technical: Postmortems on shipping bugs, Plan reviews on roadmap decisions, FAQs on CLI behaviour, Diff reviews on PRs, ADRs on design tradeoffs.

## Exceptions

`slop/uppercase-body` is whitelisted for short manifestos and on quarterly "What we cut" lists — the brand reaches for caps deliberately as a register signal. The detector should not "fix" these.
