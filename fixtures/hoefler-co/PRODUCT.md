# Brand · Hoefler & Co

## What this is

A digital type foundry that designs and licenses typefaces for editorial, identity, and screen use. The foundry's catalogue (Gotham, Mercury, Sentinel, Whitney, Sentinel Sans, Operator) is among the most widely-licensed in publishing and brand work. The public surface is the catalogue, specimen pages, licensing flow, and a small set of long-form essays on typographic history and craft.

## Voice

**Axis: meticulous-craft** (historically-grounded, specific about typographic decisions, assumes the reader cares).

Sentences are long when explaining a design choice (cite the historical model, the optical-sizing reason, the texture-on-page rationale); short when stating an inventory ("Sentinel ships in five weights and three grades"). The brand assumes the reader is a designer who already values typography — there's no "what is a serif" hedging in the prose.

When `voice` choices arise: prefer the typographic-historical framing over the marketing framing. *"Mercury was drawn in 1996 for use in newspaper editorial settings at small text sizes, where its narrow proportions and high x-height keep prose readable at 8.5pt"* beats *"Mercury is a versatile, readable serif"*.

## Audience

- **Primary**: working designers (editorial, brand, product) selecting typefaces for a project; design directors and creative leads making catalogue decisions for a client.
- **Secondary**: type-curious students and educators; design-history enthusiasts; brand owners evaluating the foundry's identity work (Mercury at the *New York Times Magazine*, Gotham on Obama '08).

Primary audience reads specimen pages in the browser, downloads trial fonts to InDesign / Figma, and licenses through the foundry's flow. The specimen page is the canonical product surface.

## Format default

**publishing** — most public artifacts are publishing-shaped: specimen pages with running text in the typeface itself, essays on typographic history, catalogue overviews. Identity case studies (the foundry's commissioned work) lean toward the same shape.

## Exceptions

- The brand uses typographic terminology directly (ligature, x-height, optical size, grade, hinting, kerning pair). Don't soften these in `simplify` — the audience expects the vocabulary.
- Decimal-aligned tables for specimen pricing are part of the register, not a generic data-table chrome decision.
