# Brand · Stratechery

## What this is

A subscription analysis publication on tech business strategy, written by Ben Thompson. Single-author essays — "Aggregation Theory", platform analysis, earnings interpretations, M&A frameworks — published weekly behind a paywall. Free Monday post + paid Tuesday–Thursday issues. The site is the product; there is no app, no dashboard, no other surface.

## Voice

**Axis: technical-analytical** (precise, structural, frameworks-first).

Sentences are long when building a framework, short when stating a conclusion. The author's name carries the byline weight — no hedge-via-passive-voice, no "many would argue". When an argument is unfinished, the prose says so explicitly ("I'm still working through this").

When `voice` choices arise (Postmortem reflection, FAQ phrasings, Plan-review prose): prefer the structural framing ("the aggregation dynamic explains why…") over the anecdotal one. Cite the framework name when invoking it; assume the reader either knows it or will follow the link.

## Audience

- **Primary**: tech executives, founders, product leaders, investors who read for strategic frame-of-reference.
- **Secondary**: journalists, analysts, students of platform economics; tech-curious generalists tracking the industry.

Primary audience reads in the browser (essays are long; no app reader). Email delivery is the secondary path; the canonical surface is the web page.

## Format default

**publishing** — most artifacts are essay-shaped analysis. Postmortems on platform mistakes (Twitter, Facebook), Plan reviews on corporate strategy shifts (Microsoft acquisitions, Apple Vision), Comparisons of platform dynamics. The brand also produces the *Stratechery Plus* bundle (Sharp Tech podcast notes, Greatest of All Talk transcripts) but the default format is the long essay.

## Exceptions

- The brand's voice tolerates long sentences and unhedged claims that an editor at a mainstream publication would soften. Don't sand the prose into "Atlantic-style" hedging when the source brand commits to a position.
- Footnotes are honoured (the brand uses them heavily); don't strip them in `simplify`.
