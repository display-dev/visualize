# Uber Style

_Independent reference inspired by Uber's public product and marketplace surfaces. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Uber._

## §1 Canonical Canvas

Uber style is a black-and-white operating surface for moving people and things. It spans rides, delivery, freight, business travel, drivers, couriers, Reserve, marketplace pricing, and autonomous mobility/delivery. The design language is restrained because the product is transactional: where from, where to, how much, when, who is moving it, and what happens next.

Use pure black, white, grays, pill CTAs, route lines, map panels, form cards, phone UI, editorial transportation photography or simple product objects. Do not invent a bright accent palette. Uber's confidence comes from clarity and scale, not decoration.

### global-header

Use a plain global nav: Ride, Drive, Deliver, Eat, Uber for Business, Freight, About, Help, Log in, Sign up. Header variants can be black-on-white or white-on-black depending on canvas. Labels stay direct.

### ride-request-hero

The canonical hero pairs a big direct headline with pickup and destination inputs, date/time, product selection, and See prices. The form is practical and high-contrast, not ornamental.

### pickup-destination-form

The form uses stacked location rows, pickup pin, destination marker, saved places, and a black pill action. Inputs sit in soft gray containers on light or lifted gray containers on dark.

### ride-option-card

Ride option cards show UberX, Comfort, Black, Green, XL, Share, or taxi where available. Include ETA, seats, price, capacity, and short fit label.

### upfront-pricing-card

Pricing cards should explain that riders see prices before confirming. Show route, trip type, time, distance, demand, fees, and final price. Keep price certainty visible.

### reserve-ride-card

Reserve cards show scheduled pickup, airport support, product eligibility, pickup protection where applicable, and cancellation context. Use calendar/time controls and a confidence-oriented CTA.

### airport-flow-card

Airport rides show terminal, pickup zone, flight tracking, baggage note, wait time, and Reserve or pickup instructions. This is a navigation problem, so maps matter.

### map-route-panel

Maps are clean and functional: route line, pickup/destination pins, ETA, driver position, venue zones, and trip card overlay. Avoid decorative map textures.

### live-trip-card

Live trip cards show driver, vehicle, plate, rating, ETA, route progress, safety tools, share trip, and contact controls.

### safety-tool-card

Safety cards show PIN verification, RideCheck, emergency help, trusted contacts, share trip, and support. Use explicit labels and strong contrast.

### driver-earnings-card

Driver surfaces show trip offers, upfront fare, distance to pickup, estimated time, tips, promotions, cash out, and earnings summary. Keep money and constraints transparent.

### courier-delivery-card

Delivery cards show order, merchant, pickup, dropoff, distance, earnings, tip, and delivery status. Use courier workflow language, not rider language.

### eats-merchant-card

Uber Eats merchant cards show restaurant order volume, prep time, menu items, courier status, ratings, promotions, and payout. The merchant is the operational actor.

### business-travel-card

Uber for Business cards show employee travel, expense codes, policies, ride programs, Central, Health, vouchers, and reporting. Keep the dashboard sober and scannable.

### business-black-reserve-card

Business Black Reserve surfaces show executive travel, scheduled rides, airport pickups, on-time protection, spend tracking, and client visits. This is premium but still simple.

### freight-shipment-card

Freight cards show shipper, carrier, load, pickup window, delivery window, equipment, rate, route, and status. Use logistics tables and route summaries.

### autonomous-solutions-card

Autonomous Solutions cards show robotaxi, autonomous delivery, partner fleet, venue pickup, and operational support. Keep the visual grounded in mobility/delivery, not sci-fi autonomy.

### product-offerings-grid

Offerings grids connect Mobility, Delivery, Freight, Transit, bikes/scooters, restaurants, business, and driver/courier platform. Use simple tiles and clear verbs.

### marketplace-balance-card

Marketplace cards explain rider demand, driver supply, pricing, incentives, matching, reliability, and affordability. Use diagrams with real labels, not generic network art.

### promotion-surge-card

Promotion and surge states need careful labeling. Show when higher demand, incentives, or promotions apply, and separate rider price from driver earnings.

### account-wallet-card

Account cards show payment method, Uber Cash, receipts, credits, subscriptions, and business profile. Keep wallet UI plain and trustworthy.

### receipt-card

Receipts show route, fare breakdown, time, distance, tolls, tips, taxes, promotions, and support link. Use itemized rows and strong totals.

### support-help-card

Help cards show trip/order selector, issue category, refund or safety path, and status. Uber support surfaces need to be self-service and specific.

### city-availability-card

City availability cards show service area, products available, airport notes, local regulations, and pricing differences. Uber is always local.

### app-download-card

Download cards show QR, app badges, phone mockup, and one simple action. Keep it utilitarian.

### editorial-image-card

Photography should show real riders, drivers, couriers, cities, vehicles, restaurants, and airports. Use 4:3 or wide editorial crops with simple captions.

### black-promo-band

Black bands carry high-emphasis conversion: sign up to ride, drive, deliver, business, or merchant. Use white text and one white pill or black-on-white inverse pill.

#### footer-directory

Footer is black with grouped links: Company, Products, Global citizenship, Travel, Delivery, Business, Freight, Help, and language/location controls.

## §2 Palette

Uber uses a strict neutral system: black, white, soft grays, and occasional blue links. Do not add brand colors.

#### black-primary

Use black for primary CTAs, footer, promo bands, map overlays, and high-emphasis nav.

#### white-canvas

Use white for forms, product grids, receipts, help pages, and business surfaces.

#### soft-gray

Use soft gray for inputs, chips, disabled states, map cards, and tertiary pills.

#### link-blue

Use blue only for text links and help contexts where web convention matters.

## §3 Typography

Uber typography is geometric, direct, and easy to read in transactional contexts.

#### display-type

Use bold geometric display type for headlines. Sentence case, no decorative tracking.

#### body-type

Use readable text for ride details, prices, help copy, and business dashboards.

#### number-type

Prices, ETA, distance, and earnings must be large enough to scan immediately.

## §4 Composition Rules

Uber compositions start with an action and a location. Where, when, price, and status matter more than mood.

### action-first

Every major surface should expose the next action: see prices, reserve, request, accept, deliver, get started, contact sales.

### route-as-structure

Use routes, maps, timelines, and pickup/dropoff markers as structural elements.

### one-primary-pill

Each section has one dominant pill action. Secondary actions are text or light pills.

### local-context

Show city, airport, venue, pickup zone, market, or service area when relevant.

### marketplace-transparency

Keep both rider/customer price and driver/courier/merchant earning context legible when discussing pricing.

## §5 Accessibility And States

Uber surfaces are used in motion and under time pressure. Contrast and target size matter.

### focus-state

Use black or white focus rings with enough offset on both light and dark bands.

### location-error-state

Location errors need explicit messages: unavailable address, pickup outside area, missing destination, airport zone mismatch.

### price-state

Price states must show estimate, confirmed upfront price, promotion, surge, toll, tip, and final receipt clearly.

### trip-state

Trip states use labels: requested, matched, arriving, in progress, completed, canceled, delayed, failed.

## §6 Anti-Patterns

Uber style fails when it becomes colorful generic mobility branding.

### avoid-rainbow-maps

Map color must stay functional. Do not add decorative routes or gradient roads.

### avoid-lifestyle-only

Photography needs operational context: ride, delivery, driver, courier, city, airport, merchant, freight.

### avoid-hidden-price

Do not bury price, ETA, fee, or earnings details in tiny muted copy.

### avoid-soft-saas

Uber is direct marketplace infrastructure. Avoid pastel SaaS cards and abstract collaboration graphics.
