Distribution-aware publishing for the org-internal web

An institutional analysis of seven publishing-platform architectures for the org-gated artifact category, with recommendations for procurement and platform engineering teams.

Jordan Kim

Abstract

We assess seven org-gated publishing architectures against four institutional criteria: latency at p99, time-to-first-byte for cold authentication paths, total-cost-of-ownership at the 1k–10k seat band, and the operational surface required to maintain the platform end-to-end.

The dominant architecture in the surveyed cohort centralises edge-rendered HTML behind a Worker-level auth gate, with origin databases serving only mutation. The pattern delivers a median p99 of 86 ms across regions with operational overhead substantially below the multi-region Postgres alternative.

§ 1 · Introduction

The org-gated artifact as a distinct category

The institutional shift from public-by-default publishing toward access-gated artifact distribution has proceeded faster than the literature has documented. The Q1 2026 industry survey conducted by this Institute found that 83% of platform engineering teams at the 500–5,000 employee band now operate at least one org-gated artifact channel — a category that includes internal dashboards, agent-generated reports, runbook archives, and the proliferating long-tail of MCP-rendered documents.

This bulletin defines the category, surveys the architectural patterns currently in production, and offers procurement guidance for platform engineering teams evaluating new entrants. The data underlying §3 is drawn from the Institute's Q1 cohort study (n=412 organisations).
