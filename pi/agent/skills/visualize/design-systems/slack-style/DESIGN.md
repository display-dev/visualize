# Slack Style

_Independent reference inspired by Slack's public product and marketing surfaces. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Slack._

## §1 Canonical Canvas

Slack style is a warm Work OS: people, channels, apps, business data, and AI agents working in the same conversational space. The current hero promise is direct: "All your people and AI agents working together." Slackbot multiplies what teams can do. The design language should show work moving from conversation to action without forcing users into another tool.

Use aubergine as the anchor, cream and lavender as friendly page canvases, white cards for product proof, and Slack's four-color identity as small functional accents. Keep the product UI detailed enough to feel like Slack: channels, message threads, huddles, canvases, lists, workflows, search, agents, and app notifications.

### global-header

The header includes Features, Solutions, Enterprise, Resources, Pricing, Sign in, Request a demo, and Get started. Menus are grouped by work mode: Collaboration, CRM, Project Management, Platform, Intelligence, Admin and Security. Use clear labels and compact descriptions.

### ai-work-platform-hero

The hero centers people and AI agents. Use a large, warm headline, aubergine or cream ground, and a product collage showing Ask Slackbot, Plan launches, Run projects, Chat with clients, and Automate tasks. Primary CTA is Get started; Find your plan or Request a demo is secondary.

### slackbot-agent-card

Slackbot is a personal agent for work, not a mascot. Show a natural prompt, context pulled from channels/files/calendar/Salesforce, and an action: schedule meeting, draft update, create canvas, summarize project, or prepare account briefing.

### agentforce-card

Agentforce cards show AI agents working inside channels with permissions and business context. Pair the agent response with source chips and an action button. The visual should say "trusted work assistant," not generic chatbot.

### ai-summary-card

AI summaries are compact catch-up blocks with decisions, action items, owners, and linked messages. Use them for missed channels, huddles, files, and daily recaps. Each summary needs traceable context.

### channel-sidebar

Slack sidebars use workspace name, channel sections, DMs, apps, and unread badges. Keep channel names human and work-specific. Sidebar color can be aubergine or a light neutral depending on canvas.

### message-thread

Message threads show avatars, timestamps, reactions, mentions, file previews, and replies. Make conversations scannable with clear indentation, not chat bubbles.

### huddle-module

Huddles show audio/video participants, screen sharing, notes, transcript, and AI note-taking. The card should feel live and lightweight, not like a formal meeting app.

### canvas-doc

Canvas components are rich, flexible docs inside Slack: title, checklist, embedded file, table, owner, and comments. Use clean document styling with Slack context around it.

### list-project-board

Lists organize work. Show tasks, owners, due dates, status, and linked channels. It should look like project management inside conversation, not a separate kanban product.

### workflow-builder-card

Workflow Builder surfaces use triggers, steps, approvals, and app actions. The new AI workflow generation pattern starts from a prompt, then produces an editable flow.

### enterprise-search-bar

Search is a cross-workspace answer system. Use a wide search field, natural-language question, answer card, and source results across messages, canvases, files, and apps.

### slack-connect-card

Slack Connect shows external partner channels, org badges, shared docs, and trust boundaries. Use labels that make cross-company work legible.

### app-integration-grid

Integrations are a major proof point. Show Salesforce, Jira, Google Drive, ChatGPT, GitHub, Calendar, and other apps as actionable tiles with event previews. Slack connects over 2,600 tools, so density is acceptable.

### crm-in-slack-card

CRM cards show account health, opportunity updates, customer context, and next steps in-channel. Salesforce should feel embedded into conversation, not bolted on.

### customer-service-card

Service workflows show case swarms, owner handoffs, customer history, and resolution state. Use channels and agent recommendations together.

### engineering-channel-card

Engineering cards show incident channel, GitHub pull request, deploy notification, code review agent, and status update. Keep it operational and direct.

### marketing-launch-card

Marketing launch cards show campaign channel, assets, approvals, task list, and huddle notes. Use lively accent color but keep the workflow obvious.

### sales-room-card

Sales rooms show account team, customer thread, Salesforce context, proposal file, mutual action plan, and Slackbot prep. Enterprise sales needs shared context and next actions.

### template-gallery-card

Templates help teams start work fast. Show task management, scale, engagement, trust, launch, incident, sales, and support templates as compact cards.

### marketplace-card

Slack Marketplace cards include apps and agents. Show category, install state, permissions, rating or use case, and primary action. Keep agent discovery distinct from app discovery.

### whats-new-card

What's New cards cover Slackbot Skills, email/calendar actions, scheduled tasks, product releases, and feature enhancements. Use a small news-card rhythm with clear badges.

### trusted-logo-strip

Trusted-by strips are simple: IBM, OpenAI, Stripe, Rivian, Spotify Advertising, Wayfair, Celonis, Delivery Hero, HelloFresh, and others. Logos should support trust without overwhelming the product story.

### pricing-plan-card

Pricing cards include Free, Pro, Business+, and Enterprise options. Show message history, app integrations, huddles, external messages, AI level, admin/security, and support. Business+ is the natural place for advanced AI emphasis.

### feature-comparison-table

The comparison table is dense and practical. Rows include message history, integrations, huddles, Slack Connect, AI summaries, Slackbot, workflow generation, AI search, daily recaps, file summaries, SSO, SCIM, EMM, DLP, support, and compliance.

### security-admin-card

Admin and security cards show roles, permissions, Enterprise Key Management, data loss prevention, audit logs, and access controls. Trust is crucial when AI can see workplace context.

### footer-directory

The footer is a broad product directory with Product, Why Slack, Features, Solutions, Resources, Company, region selector, Download Slack, and legal links. Keep it structured and low-drama.

## §2 Palette

Slack's palette is aubergine-led with cream/lavender warmth and small four-color accents. It should feel social and enterprise-ready at the same time.

#### aubergine-primary

Use deep aubergine for primary CTAs, footer, featured pricing, sidebar, and high-emphasis panels. It is the brand anchor.

#### cream-lavender-canvas

Use cream, lavender, and soft pastel grounds for marketing and Work OS pages. These canvases make dense workplace concepts feel approachable.

#### slack-accents

Use blue, green, yellow, and red as small product accents: unread badges, reactions, app marks, status dots, and data highlights. Do not make the page rainbow.

#### link-blue

Links and secondary interactive text can use a confident Slack blue. Keep primary conversion actions aubergine.

## §3 Typography

Slack typography is friendly, rounded, and clear. Large display headlines feel warm; product UI labels stay precise.

#### display-type

Use a humanist display sans for headlines, with strong weight and comfortable line-height. Avoid harsh condensed tech type.

#### ui-type

Use a readable sans for messages, labels, tables, and menus. Slack UI contains many small words; clarity wins.

#### message-type

Messages should look conversational: avatar, sender, time, text, reaction. Do not use bubble-heavy consumer chat styling.

## §4 Composition Rules

Slack composition should show context and action in the same frame. Conversation is the operating surface.

### conversation-to-action

Show a message or prompt leading to an action: a workflow, task, canvas, meeting note, CRM update, or agent response.

### context-stays-visible

When AI answers, keep source context visible: channel, file, canvas, app, or Salesforce record. This makes AI trustworthy.

### app-inside-channel

Apps should appear as embedded channel objects, not disconnected logo cards. Notifications, buttons, and fields should look actionable.

### friendly-density

Slack pages can be dense, but spacing and pastel grounds keep them friendly. Avoid hard dashboard grids.

### work-os-loop

A full Slack story loops through channels, canvases, lists, apps, AI, and workflows. Show at least two of these together in major sections.

## §5 Accessibility And States

Slack surfaces need strong text contrast, visible focus, and clear status labels because the product carries real operational work.

### focus-state

Use clear aubergine or blue outlines with enough offset on buttons, links, inputs, and message actions.

### unread-state

Unread channels use badges, bold text, and color. Do not rely only on a tiny dot.

### agent-permission-state

AI and agent outputs need permission and source cues. Show what the answer used and what it can access.

### loading-state

Loading should resemble message skeletons, summary cards, search results, or workflow steps.

## §6 Anti-Patterns

Slack style breaks when it turns into generic purple SaaS or ignores the workspace surface.

### avoid-chat-bubbles

Slack messages are workspace threads, not consumer bubble chat.

### avoid-ai-magic

Slackbot must be grounded in channels, files, calendar, apps, and permissions. Avoid floating sparkle AI.

### avoid-rainbow-overuse

The four-color identity is for accents and marks. Do not turn every section into a color sampler.

### avoid-empty-collaboration

Collaboration visuals need owners, channels, tasks, files, or decisions. Generic avatars around a chart are weak.
