# WIRED Style

_Independent reference inspired by WIRED's public editorial surfaces. Not affiliated with or endorsed by WIRED or Condé Nast._

## §1 Canonical Canvas

WIRED style is a black-and-white editorial system for explaining what comes next. Its current public framing says WIRED is obsessed with rigorous investigations and reporting that map the people, products, and ideas shaping the future. The surface is not a startup site: it is a magazine, news desk, reviews desk, newsletter network, video studio, and subscription product.

Use a stark canvas, strong masthead, sharp story hierarchy, serif-led headlines, sans metadata, hairline dividers, image-led story cards, and almost no decorative color. The brand should feel skeptical, literate, and fast enough for daily news while still carrying print-magazine authority.

### masthead-bar

The masthead is the primary brand signal. Center the WIRED wordmark, keep the surrounding chrome sparse, and let black-on-white contrast do the work.

### section-navigation

Navigation should include Security, Politics, The Big Story, Business, Science, Culture, Reviews, newsletters, podcasts, video, magazine, events, insider, consulting, and search.

### todays-picks-grid

Today's Picks use a mixed hierarchy: one large story, several smaller story cards, labels, headlines, bylines, and images. The grid should feel curated by editors, not algorithmically uniform.

### lead-story-card

Lead cards need a strong image, category kicker, high-contrast headline, dek, byline, and enough whitespace to signal importance.

### story-row

Story rows use image, category, headline, brief summary, byline, and a thin divider. Keep rows crisp and scannable.

### category-front

Category fronts should feel like desks: Security, Politics, Science, Business, Culture, Gear, Reviews, and Artificial Intelligence each carry their own reporting rhythm but share the same monochrome skeleton.

### big-story-package

Big Story packages are more ambitious than ordinary posts. Use larger imagery, longer headline rhythm, subnavigation, related links, and a sense of editorial weight.

### subscriber-exclusive-card

Subscriber cards show lock or exclusivity state, story label, headline, dek, byline, and subscription prompt. The paywall should feel editorial, not commerce-heavy.

### wired-classics-card

Classics modules surface archive stories with original publication context, condensed summaries, and a clear historical marker.

### reviews-buying-guide

Reviews and buying guides show tested products, verdicts, product images, category labels, affiliate disclosure, and updated dates. Gear can be more service-oriented than news pages.

### product-review-card

Product review cards should include product name, score or verdict if present, pros/cons, price context, retailer links, and testing notes.

### newsletter-signup-panel

Newsletter panels show newsletter name, short promise, frequency, email field, privacy note, and subscribe action. Keep form design square and plain.

### livestream-ama-card

Livestream and AMA cards show topic, host names, time, submission prompt, and sign-in or subscriber requirement when relevant.

### video-story-card

Video cards show thumbnail, series type such as Tech Support or Autocomplete Interview, duration, title, and play affordance. Avoid turning video modules into streaming-service chrome.

### podcast-card

Podcast cards show title, host, episode topic, date, platform links, and follow action. Keep them editorially aligned with story modules.

### author-byline-card

Byline cards show author, role, story count, and social or contact links only when useful. The author is a journalistic signal.

### article-header

Article headers use category, headline, dek, byline, date, image, caption, and share/save actions. The headline should dominate.

### longform-body

Longform body copy should use a readable serif, generous line height, pull quotes, inline links, image captions, and occasional subheads.

### pull-quote

Pull quotes should be typographic, high-contrast, and sparse. Do not add colored quote marks or ornamental framing.

### image-caption

Captions are small, factual, and separated from article prose. Include photographer or source credit where needed.

### related-stories-rail

Related rails show headline, category, thumbnail, and a compact reason to continue reading. Keep borders thin.

### account-subscription-panel

Account panels support subscribe, sign in, digital access, magazine archive, saved stories, newsletters, app access, and customer support.

### rss-directory

RSS and feed directories list Top Stories plus Business, Artificial Intelligence, Culture, Gear, Ideas, Science, Security, and other desks with concise descriptions.

#### international-selector

International selectors should be plain, listing editions such as United States, Italia, Japan, and regional properties without decorative country graphics.

### footer-directory

Footer groups More From WIRED, Reviews and Guides, Condé Nast links, policies, accessibility, social channels, and copyright. Keep it text-heavy and utilitarian.

## §2 Palette

WIRED color is mostly the absence of color. Black, white, gray, and occasional link blue are enough.

#### white-paper

Use white as the default page and article canvas. It should evoke paper and make images carry the visual color.

#### black-ink

Use pure or near black for masthead, headlines, rules, primary buttons, footer bands, and strong editorial emphasis.

#### hairline-gray

Use pale gray for dividers, grid rules, article separators, and muted interface borders.

#### link-blue

Use blue only for inline links, utility links, or selected digital affordances. It is not a brand accent for cards.

## §3 Typography

WIRED depends on editorial typography more than color or motion.

#### condensed-display-serif

Use a tall, condensed, high-contrast display face for major headlines and package titles. It should feel magazine-born.

#### readable-body-serif

Use a humanist serif for article prose, long deks, captions in narrative context, and byline-adjacent material.

#### utility-sans

Use a clean sans for navigation, labels, metadata, buttons, product review details, newsletter forms, and account pages.

#### strict-case-labels

Category labels and metadata can use uppercase or compact title case. Keep spacing measured and avoid decorative tracking.

## §4 Composition Rules

The layout is a news product. It should look edited.

### hierarchy-over-symmetry

Use asymmetrical editorial hierarchy. A lead story can be much larger than secondary items.

### rule-based-separation

Use thin rules instead of shadows, cards, or tinted containers. Lines make the page feel like a magazine grid.

### image-and-headline

Story modules are built from image and headline first. Summaries, bylines, and metadata support them.

### desk-based-grouping

Group content by editorial desk. Security, Politics, Science, Business, Culture, Gear, and Reviews should remain visible as organizing logic.

### subscription-visible

Show subscriber exclusives, sign-in state, newsletters, archive access, and app access clearly. The subscription layer is part of the product.

### commerce-with-disclosure

Reviews and buying guides may include commerce links, but affiliate disclosure and testing context must stay visible.

### restrained-motion

Use little motion. Hover states can underline, darken, or reveal image overlays, but never behave like a social feed.

## §5 Accessibility And States

High-contrast editorial design still needs clear digital states.

### focus-state

Use black focus outlines on light canvas and white outlines on dark footer or masthead areas.

### link-state

Links need default, hover, visited, and active states. Inline links should remain distinguishable from surrounding prose.

### paywall-state

Article states include free, metered, subscriber-only, sign-in required, saved, and archive access.

### story-freshness-state

Show published date, updated date, original publication date, and archive context where relevant.

### review-state

Review states include tested, updated, deal, discontinued, affiliate, best overall, budget pick, and upgrade pick.

## §6 Anti-Patterns

WIRED style fails when it becomes generic technology branding.

### avoid-saas-cards

Do not turn story grids into rounded feature cards with icons. This is a publication.

### avoid-color-systems

Do not add category color palettes, gradients, or neon tech accents. Editorial hierarchy carries the system.

### avoid-soft-brand-voice

Copy should be sharp, reported, and specific. Avoid motivational technology slogans.

### avoid-hidden-commerce

Reviews must keep testing basis, affiliate disclosure, and purchase context visible.
