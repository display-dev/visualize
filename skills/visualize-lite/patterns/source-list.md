# Source List

## Use when

Use source lists when evidence provenance affects trust, recency, or reproducibility.

## Do not use when

Do not create fake citations, placeholder sources, or ornamental source entries for common knowledge.

## Semantic shape

Use ordered lists for footnotes, unordered lists for grouped sources, and links with visible titles. Include publisher/date when recency matters.

## Annotated sources

When comparing sources, place a short takeaway beside each source identity so the reader can find the relevant pattern. Group longer facts by concept; preserve the full account in the reading flow or accessible disclosure within SKILL.md's scope. Keep evidence limitations visible beside the source and distinguish external observations from the artifact's proposed choices. This recipe owns provenance and annotation; [table.md](table.md) owns tabular comparison structure. Ordinary citations remain ordinary citations, without a forced overview or disclosure layer.

## Type roles

Source titles should be readable. URLs and access details are secondary. Keep footnote numbers calm and consistent.

## Mobile behavior

Long URLs break safely. Source entries stack without oversized chrome.

## Failure modes

- Fabricated citations.
- URL-only source lists with no title or context.
- Source entries louder than the claims they support.
