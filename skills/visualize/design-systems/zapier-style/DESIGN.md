# Zapier Style

_Independent reference inspired by Zapier's public product, pricing, MCP, and AI orchestration surfaces. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Zapier._

## §1 Canonical Canvas

Zapier style is a warm automation platform for turning apps, data, forms, workflows, and AI into operational systems. The current product story is AI orchestration: workflows, agents, chatbots, tables, forms, canvas, functions, MCP, templates, enterprise controls, and thousands of app connections.

Use warm cream surfaces, deep coffee ink, saturated orange actions, rounded-but-practical cards, app icons, connected workflow diagrams, task quotas, template grids, governance controls, and proof that automation is observable. The brand should feel useful and empowering, not robotic.

### global-navigation

Navigation groups Products, Solutions, Resources, Pricing, Enterprise, Contact Sales, app exploration, templates, and sign-up. Product menus include Zaps, Tables, Forms, Canvas, Agents, Chatbots, Functions, MCP, and app integrations.

### ai-orchestration-hero

Hero sections should name AI orchestration directly. Pair the headline with connected nodes, workflow cards, app icons, agent actions, and controls for secure, observable, compliant automation.

### toolkit-tabs

Toolkit tabs show AI Workflows, AI Agents, AI Chatbots, Tables, Forms, Canvas, Enterprise, Functions, MCP, and app count. The active tab should reveal a real product job.

### zap-workflow-card

Workflow cards show trigger, actions, conditional paths, AI processing, filters, formatter, webhooks, schedule, test, publish, and history state.

### ai-agent-card

Agent cards show goal, connected apps, knowledge source, approvals, action permissions, run history, and human review. Agents should feel governed.

### chatbot-card

Chatbot cards show customer question, answer source, escalation path, conversation log, connected actions, and embed channel.

### table-record-card

Tables cards show records, fields, linked workflows, AI fields, buttons, views, owner, and last run. Tables are data that automations can read and update.

### form-capture-card

Forms cards show questions, conditional logic, submission state, validation, routing action, and connected table or workflow.

### canvas-map-card

Canvas cards show process map, branches, connected apps, AI suggestions, workflow readiness, and implementation steps.

### mcp-action-card

MCP cards show AI client, connected apps, permitted actions, tool call, history log, task usage, and governance. The message is AI can act, not only talk.

### functions-card

Functions cards show code editor, function input, output, logs, environment, beta state, and where custom code extends a workflow.

### app-directory-grid

App directory grids show app icons, categories, app names, connected account state, available triggers/actions, and popular templates.

### template-card

Template cards show source app, destination app, AI step if any, use case, task saved, and start action. Templates should be specific and operational.

### use-case-strip

Use-case strips show IT tickets, lead routing, employee onboarding, sales coaching, weekly communications, release notes, and review responses.

### enterprise-control-panel

Enterprise panels show role-based permissions, app restrictions, workspace controls, managed connections, SSO, SCIM, audit trails, and central AI oversight.

### security-compliance-card

Security cards show SOC 2, SOC 3, GDPR, CCPA, credential infrastructure, data handling, and compliance evidence without burying it in footnotes.

### approval-state-card

Approval cards show Slack approval, human-in-the-loop decision, approve, reject, edit, retry, and escalation. AI speed still needs checkpoints.

### history-log-card

History cards show who automated what, app, action, timestamp, status, task usage, input, output, and replay or troubleshoot controls.

### pricing-task-slider

Pricing sliders show task tier, monthly/yearly billing, currency, plan cards, task limits, Free/Professional/Team/Enterprise, and add-ons.

### plan-card

Plan cards show included products, task allowance, AI features, support, premium apps, webhooks, governance, and calls to try or contact sales.

### customer-proof-card

Proof cards show company logo, quote, concrete automation outcome, saved time, tickets solved, records managed, and read-story action.

### ai-template-grid

AI template grids show Claude, Perplexity, ChatGPT, Gemini, Jasper, Google Forms, Slack, Asana, Notion, Pinecone, and other app combinations.

### guided-setup-flow

Setup flows show connect app, authenticate, choose trigger, map fields, test sample, publish workflow, monitor history.

### footer-directory

Footer directories group Product, Solutions by team, app categories, use cases, how-to resources, AI resources, developer resources, company, and legal.

## §2 Palette

Zapier color is warm cream, coffee ink, and orange action. Do not cool it down.

#### cream-canvas

Use cream rather than pure white for page background, hero surfaces, pricing pages, and product explanations.

#### coffee-ink

Use warm dark brown-black for text, borders, diagrams, and structural UI.

#### zapier-orange

Use saturated orange for primary CTAs, active steps, important workflow nodes, and brand stamps. Keep it purposeful.

#### soft-cream-panel

Use deeper cream for bands, comparison areas, template cards, and workflow canvas backgrounds.

#### app-icon-color

Let app icons bring secondary color. Avoid inventing a broad brand accent system.

## §3 Typography

Zapier typography is friendly but work-focused.

#### degular-display

Use a rounded display sans for big brand moments. Keep it confident, warm, and not too corporate.

#### inter-product

Use Inter or a neutral sans for body, forms, labels, pricing, workflow controls, and dense product UI.

#### compact-ui-labels

Labels should be compact and concrete: Trigger, Action, Path, Approval, Task usage, History, Connected apps.

#### plain-benefit-copy

Copy should explain the job: automate support tickets, route leads, summarize meetings, update records, connect AI to apps.

## §4 Composition Rules

Zapier layouts should show systems being connected.

### connection-first

Use nodes, app icons, arrows, triggers, actions, tables, forms, and approvals. The visual logic is connection.

### products-work-together

Show Zaps, Tables, Forms, Canvas, MCP, Agents, Chatbots, and Functions as parts of one system, not isolated add-ons.

### start-from-template

Templates should appear early for common workflows. Zapier's promise is fast start, not blank-canvas intimidation.

### governed-ai

Whenever AI agents or MCP act, show permissions, controls, logs, and workspace policy.

### warm-operational-density

Keep the canvas warm and approachable while allowing enough density for real workflow data.

### proof-with-metrics

Customer proof should include measurable work saved or automated, not generic trust logos alone.

## §5 Accessibility And States

Automation UI has many states; make them visible.

### focus-state

Use orange or coffee focus rings with strong contrast on cream and dark coffee surfaces.

### workflow-state

Workflow states include draft, testing, published, paused, errored, replayed, skipped, waiting approval, and complete.

### connection-state

Connection states include connected, expired, needs reauth, restricted, managed, shared, and disconnected.

### task-state

Task states include included, consumed, over limit, queued, successful, failed, replayed, and billed.

### mcp-state

MCP states include client connected, app allowed, action blocked, history logged, workspace disabled, and task charged.

## §6 Anti-Patterns

Zapier style fails when it becomes generic orange SaaS.

### avoid-empty-automation-claims

Do not say automate everything without showing triggers, actions, apps, data, or logs.

### avoid-ai-without-controls

AI agents and MCP need permissions, approvals, and history. Do not make them magical black boxes.

### avoid-cool-gray-dashboard

Do not replace cream and coffee warmth with default gray admin UI.

### avoid-icon-confetti

App icons should explain integrations and workflow context, not become decoration.
