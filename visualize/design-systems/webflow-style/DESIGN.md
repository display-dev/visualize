# Webflow Style

_Independent reference inspired by Webflow's public website and product surfaces. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Webflow._

## §1 Canonical Canvas

Webflow style is a bright visual website platform with a black editorial core and vivid product-category accents. The current product story is broader than a site builder: Webflow calls itself a website experience platform for visual site building, composable CMS, analytics, optimization, localization, enterprise control, apps, APIs, AI, AEO, and code components.

Use a white canvas, near-black type, tight rectangular controls, large product screenshots, precise UI crop-ins, and saturated accent fields for product modules. The brand should feel like creative control at enterprise scale: designers can build, marketers can publish and optimize, and teams can govern the system.

### global-navigation

Navigation uses Product, Solutions, Resources, Enterprise, Pricing, login, contact sales, and start-free actions. Mega menus can segment audiences by marketer, designer, developer, agency, and enterprise.

### platform-hero

Hero sections should name the platform promise directly: build, manage, and optimize web experiences. Pair the headline with real builder, CMS, analytics, or optimization UI, not abstract web shapes.

### visual-canvas-panel

The visual canvas is the signature surface. Show layers, breakpoints, style controls, page structure, component variants, interactions, and a live page preview.

### site-builder-card

Site-builder cards show custom layouts, generated clean code, flex/grid controls, animation timelines, and custom-code escape hatches. Keep them visual-first.

### designer-toolbar

Designer toolbars should include navigator, pages, components, variables, style panel, add element, publish, preview, and breakpoint controls. Density is acceptable because the product is a professional tool.

### component-canvas-card

Component cards show reusable variants side by side, cascading style changes, component properties, and approval state. The component system should feel visual, not code-only.

### cms-model-card

CMS cards show collections, fields, references, item status, scheduled publishing, locales, and API access. Webflow's CMS is composable and visual-first.

### content-editor-card

Content editing cards show non-technical publishing: title, slug, SEO metadata, rich content, dynamic fields, draft/review/publish states, and permissions.

### localization-workspace

Localization surfaces show locale switchers, translated static pages, localized CMS items, localized images, alt text, metadata, subdirectories, and routing. Treat localization as a design workflow, not a translation table.

### analyze-dashboard

Analyze cards show native visitor behavior, top pages, traffic sources, bounce rate, goal clicks, and performance insight. Use charts sparingly and tie metrics back to pages.

### optimize-experiment-card

Optimize cards show A/B tests, AI-powered personalization, target audience, conversion goal, variant preview, confidence, and winner state.

### seo-aeo-panel

SEO and AEO panels show sitewide audits, metadata, Open Graph, schema hints, search discoverability, AI-search suggestions, and action items.

### ai-site-builder-card

AI builder cards show prompt, generated sitemap, page sections, design system reuse, and editable output. AI should accelerate the canvas, not replace craft.

### ai-copy-card

AI copy cards show first drafts, content variants, CMS item generation, brand guardrails, and human review. Keep generated content visibly editable.

### code-components-card

Code Components cards show React component generation, props, preview, reuse, and handoff to production code. This is where Webflow crosses from visual design into engineering.

### figma-plugin-card

Figma-to-Webflow cards show imported frames, class mapping, reusable components, and production-ready code. Avoid treating Figma as a static screenshot.

### devlink-card

DevLink cards show visual components exported for React, component names, props, sync status, and developer handoff. Keep the bridge explicit.

### webflow-cloud-card

Cloud cards show app hosting, code, deployment status, environment variables, logs, and integration with Webflow sites. Present it as an expansion of the website platform.

### enterprise-control-panel

Enterprise panels show workspaces, roles, permissions, publishing workflows, design approvals, security, compliance, SSO, and governance.

### hosting-card

Hosting cards show fast managed hosting, SSL, CDN, redirects, domains, uptime, scaling, and publish status. Keep reliability factual.

### marketplace-grid

Marketplace layouts show templates, experts, apps, plugins, categories, filters, and ratings. Use dense browse patterns with strong visual thumbnails.

### customer-proof-strip

Proof strips show recognizable customer logos and concrete outcomes such as conversion lift, speed to market, cost savings, traffic growth, and form fills.

### made-in-webflow-gallery

Made in Webflow galleries should be image-led. Show real site screenshots, maker names, categories like CMS or interactions, and visit links.

### pricing-plan-card

Pricing cards show free start, site plans, workspace plans, localization, ecommerce, CMS limits, bandwidth, roles, and enterprise contact. Use comparison density.

#### university-docs-panel

Education panels show Webflow University, help docs, lessons, docs links, and support resources. Treat education as part of the product experience.

#### footer-directory

Footer directories can be broad: Product, Solutions, Resources, Enterprise, Marketplace, Community, Developers, Company, and legal. Keep link groups crisp.

## §2 Palette

The palette is black and white first, then saturated category color. Color identifies product areas and moments of action; it should not wash over every section.

#### white-canvas

Use white or near-white for most marketing and product explanation pages. It gives screenshots and category colors room to work.

#### near-black-ink

Use near-black for headlines, primary CTAs, nav, and serious enterprise surfaces. Avoid gray hero headlines.

#### category-purple

Use vivid purple for design, AI, component, or platform emphasis when a module needs brand energy.

#### product-accent-set

Use pink, blue, orange, green, and yellow as bounded module accents for CMS, optimization, localization, ecommerce, and marketplace categories.

#### elevated-gray

Use cool light gray for panels, dividers, editor chrome, screenshot backgrounds, and comparison tables. Keep borders thin.

## §3 Typography

Webflow typography is bold, compact, and editorial without becoming decorative.

#### visual-sans-display

Use a clean geometric sans for display at large sizes with medium or semibold weight. Let scale create impact.

#### compact-section-heading

Section headings can be large but should stay crisp and direct. Do not use gradient text or decorative letter spacing.

#### interface-labels

Product UI labels use small, dense, high-contrast sans text. Labels should feel like an editor, not a marketing brochure.

#### monospace-technical

Use monospace only for code, API, CSS class names, environment variables, or developer handoff fields.

## §4 Composition Rules

Webflow compositions should show creative freedom inside controlled systems.

### canvas-before-card

Lead with the visual canvas or a live site preview whenever possible. Cards are supporting structure, not the primary brand signal.

### product-modules-as-tabs

Use tabs for Site builder, CMS, Optimize, Analyze, Localization, Management, Apps, and APIs. The current platform story is modular.

### screenshot-as-proof

Use real-feeling UI screenshots and cropped product surfaces. Abstract illustrations are weaker than visible controls and page output.

### category-color-bands

Use vivid color bands for major product sections or feature categories. Keep the rest of the page monochrome so the colors keep meaning.

### enterprise-and-creator

Balance creator freedom with controls for scale: approvals, roles, publishing, security, analytics, and workflow state.

### interaction-visible

Show arrows, preview controls, publish states, locale switchers, and experiment variants. The product should feel operated.

### marketplace-visual-density

Marketplace and gallery sections should use image grids with metadata. Avoid generic three-card feature blocks.

## §5 Accessibility And States

Because the style uses sharp black-white contrast and saturated accents, state design has to be deliberate.

### focus-state

Use a visible black or purple focus ring with enough offset from white, gray, and saturated panels.

### publish-state

Publish states include draft, staged, review, scheduled, live, localized, experiment running, and archived.

### role-state

Role states include owner, designer, editor, marketer, developer, agency, approver, and viewer.

### localization-state

Locale states include source, translated, needs review, missing metadata, custom image, hidden element, and routed.

### experiment-state

Optimization states include draft, running, collecting data, winner, personalized, paused, and rolled back.

## §6 Anti-Patterns

Webflow style fails when it loses the actual builder and becomes generic colorful SaaS.

### avoid-generic-gradient-saas

Do not turn the accents into full-page gradients, glowing blobs, or gradient text. Use bounded product color.

### avoid-empty-creative-copy

Do not use vague creativity slogans without showing canvas controls, CMS structure, or published output.

### avoid-code-only-framing

Webflow is visual-first even when it exposes code, APIs, DevLink, or React components. Keep the visual workflow central.

### avoid-over-soft-cards

Cards should be crisp and rectangular. Large pill cards and soft bubbly shapes weaken the professional editor feel.
